Study Overview

ONT amplicon sequencing (16S & ITS) of vineyard soils across three Chilean wine regions — Paine, Elqui & Itata — exploring bacterial, fungal, and functional community structure alongside soil chemistry.

Ecological patterns
Saprotrophs dominate across all sites. Mortierella (soil saprotroph) alone accounts for 20% of all ITS reads, peaking at 73.6% in Itata/Cinsault. Pathotroph-saprotroph taxa like Alternaria, Fusarium, and Cladosporium are present but at lower abundances. Leohumicola (ericoid mycorrhizal) represents the only significant symbiotroph, found primarily in Cinsault soils.

Summary of Key Findings

Major patterns identified across bacterial, fungal, functional, and soil chemistry analyses of the vineyard soil microbiome project.

Site Differentiation
Three distinct microbiome profiles. Both 16S and ITS PCoA show complete separation of Paine (Ramsey), Elqui (Carignan), and Itata (Cinsault) with high statistical support (Procrustes M²=0.23, p=0.001; Mantel r=0.69, p=0.001).

Soil Chemistry ↔ Microbiome
27 of 150 correlations significant (FDR q<0.05). Strongest: Potasio–K_transport (ρ=0.81). Soil pH strongly shapes fungal beneficial guilds and salinity tolerance genes. Copper (Cu) inversely correlates with phytohormone-related functions, potentially reflecting historical vineyard management.
Functional Highlights

PICRUSt2 Predicted Functions

N-cycleDenitrification dominates (nosZ, norC highest in Ramsey). Nitrification (amoA/B/C) enriched in Carignan.
C-cycleCalvin cycle genes (rbcL, PRK) present across all sites. Methanotrophy (pmo genes) only detected in Carignan.
S-cycleAssimilatory sulfate reduction dominates. Sox pathway genes suggest active sulfur oxidation, particularly in Ramsey.
Top MetaCycAerobic respiration, pyruvate fermentation, and branched-chain amino acid biosynthesis most abundant predicted pathways.
Cross-Kingdom
95 strong bacteria–fungi correlations (|ρ|>0.6). Ktedonosporobacter–Saitozyma (ρ=0.92) is the strongest positive pair, both Cinsault-enriched. Mortierella shows positive associations with Gemmata and Vicinamibacter, suggesting shared ecological niches.
